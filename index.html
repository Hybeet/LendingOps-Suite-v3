💰 Total Cash Today ▶

The net physical liquid cash that must match the supervisor's physical vault balance at close of day.

Formula:
(Opening Cash + Opening PD + Cash From Office + Supposed Coll + Recovery + Interest + Forms + Cards + Pay Off + Today's PD)
MINUS
(Used PD + Bank Deposit + Default + Cost of Deals)
Business Example: If an officer comes back with field collections, any cash expenses or bank deposits must be deducted from the gross receipts to find the exact final physical cash volume.
📊 Actual Collection ▶

The true, organic financial yield recovered from the market field during active tracking cycles.

Formula:
Supposed Collection - Default + Recovery + Pay Off + Today's PD - Used PD
Business Example: If a market has a target baseline of ₦100,000 but ₦10,000 defaulted and ₦5,000 extra was brought in via recoveries, the Actual Collection matches ₦95,000.
🔄 Pay Down (PD) System ▶

Understanding Advance Payments: Opening Pay Down vs. Today's Pay Down vs. Used Pay Down.

📌 Opening PD: Surplus funds holding in a client's savings card at start of day.

📌 Today's PD: Brand new extra advance money deposited by the client today.

📌 Used PD: Actively drawing down from their card balance to pay today's daily rate instead of using cash.

🛡️ Recovery & Outstanding Logic ▶

How missed cards handle debt collection and separate isolated transactions from the cell totals.

The Core Rule: When a client defaults, their debt moves to Outstanding. When they pay back extra later, it is flagged as a Recovery.

Real Example: Daily rate = ₦3,600. Client pays ₦4,100 total. The ledger grid saves ₦4,100 to update the bank matrix, but isolates the ₦500 to stamp the sheet comment cleanly as "Recovery of ₦500".
💰 Pay Off Matrix ▶

The final lump-sum liquidation settlement processed to completely close out an active loan tracking file.

Impact Profile:
Increases Actual Collection Total & Physical Cash Balance
System Rule: Reduces Remaining Balance instantly to ₦0.
Business Example: If a client owes a remaining principal balance of ₦15,000 across their remaining 25-day cycle but decides to pay it all down immediately to close the account, this entire bulk amount is recorded as a Pay Off, terminating the tracking loop.
📅 Next Day Collection ▶

Pre-calculated installment projection metric used to prepare field officers for the upcoming morning route run.

Baseline Equation:
Sum of (Active Standard Daily Repayments) for all open non-settled accounts assigned to a specific market track.
Business Example: This metric gives the operations manager an exact target pipeline figure. If a market lane contains 10 active customers who each pay a flat ₦3,600 daily rate, the Next Day Collection baseline target will automatically flag as ₦36,000 for the morning ledger preparation.
📝 How to Prepare a Good Daily Report ▶

A clean report gives the system the right information to extract, calculate and validate your daily operations correctly.

1
Use clear headings.

Label sections such as Collection, Disbursement, Pay Down, Recovery and other major sections clearly.

2
Keep customer information together.

Put the customer's name, amount and other customer information under the correct customer.

3
Separate the customer from the guarantor.

A guarantor is not the borrower. Make sure the report clearly identifies the guarantor.

Simple rule: The clearer the report is written, the easier it is for LendingOps to understand it.
⚠️ Common Report Errors ▶

Small formatting mistakes can make the system miss a value or misunderstand what the value means.

❌ Missing customer name The system may not know who the transaction belongs to.
❌ Missing amount The system may find the customer but cannot determine the disbursement amount.
❌ Poor or unclear heading The system may not know where the Disbursement section starts.
❌ Guarantor written like a customer The system may accidentally treat the guarantor as another customer if the report structure is unclear.
❌ Blank template filled incorrectly Empty customer templates should not be mistaken for real transactions.
Correction: Do not immediately assume the system is broken. First check whether the information in the report is clearly labelled.
📥 How to Write Disbursement Correctly ▶

The Disbursement section is where the system gets new loan records. Good structure makes the records easier to detect.

Recommended structure:

Customer Name
Customer Type
Amount / Next Loan Amount
Phone Number
Other customer information
Guarantor information
Example:

Name: Example Customer (RT)

Next loan amount: ₦90,000

Phone no: 08000000000

Guarantor
Name: Example Guarantor

💡 The system does not need every piece of information to calculate the principal, but clear labels make extraction much safer.
🔍 Why Disbursement Was Not Detected ▶

If the Disbursement Core says no records were found, check these things before reporting a system problem.

1. Check the heading.

Make sure the report contains a clear Disbursement heading.

2. Check the customer name.

The customer's name should be clearly labelled or clearly identifiable.

3. Check the amount.

Use a clear amount such as Amount, Next loan amount, or another supported amount format.

4. Check where the section ends.

Make sure the Disbursement section is not mixed into the next major section such as Pay Down.

Quick fix: Correct the report text, then use the Disbursement ↻ Refresh button. You do not need to restart the entire workflow just because the Disbursement text was corrected.
👤 Customer vs Guarantor ▶

A guarantor supports the customer's loan. A guarantor is not a disbursement customer.

Customer: The person receiving the loan.

Guarantor: The person standing as guarantor for that customer.

Wrong structure:
Name: Customer
Phone: 080...
Name: Guarantor
Phone: 081...

Better structure:
Customer Name: Customer
Phone: 080...
Guarantor Name: Guarantor
Phone: 081...
💡 Always make the word Guarantor clear. This prevents the guarantor from being mistaken for another customer.
💰 Disbursement Amount & Deal Rules ▶

Learn how the system determines the principal when the report contains an amount, number of deals, or both.

Main rule:

1 Deal = ₦30,000

3 Deals = ₦90,000
4 Deals = ₦120,000
5 Deals = ₦150,000
Amount priority:

When a clear Next Loan Amount is available, it is used instead of an old/previous loan amount.

If the report gives New Deals without an amount, the system can calculate the principal using the ₦30,000-per-deal rule.

💡 Do not use a phone number as an amount. Phone numbers and financial amounts are different fields.
🆕 Returning Customer vs New Customer ▶

Customer type affects how the system matches the customer and how the new loan record is prepared.

(RT) = Returning Customer

(NW) = New Customer

If no NW marker is present, the system treats the customer as Returning Customer under the current rules.

💡 If the customer is genuinely new, make sure the report clearly identifies the customer as NW.
🧠 How DataCore Matches Customers ▶

DataCore is the registry used to check existing customer information before processing transactions.

1
Report gives the customer name.

The system reads the customer from the report.

2
DataCore checks the market.

The customer is checked against the selected market's registry.

3
Existing information is brought into the preview.

Information such as customer identity, branch and officer information can be used during enrichment.

Important: DataCore matching does not mean that every value shown in the preview must replace what the officer reviewed and edited. Editable fields remain editable.
🔄 When to Use DataCore Refresh ▶

Refresh allows the current report and current market information to be read again without restarting the entire extraction process.

Use Refresh when:

• You corrected a customer name.

• You corrected the market.

• You corrected the report date.

• DataCore customer information was not loaded correctly.

• You want to reload the current DataCore state.

💡 Refresh is different from Extract Data. Refresh is intended to reload DataCore information without unnecessarily restarting the complete report extraction workflow.
🚨 DataCore Errors & What They Mean ▶

Most DataCore problems can be understood by checking the market, customer identity, date and connection status.

Customer not found Check the spelling of the customer name and make sure the correct market was selected.
Market missing Confirm that the report contains a valid market and that the market was extracted correctly.
Report date missing Check that the report contains a clear valid date.
DataCore not loaded Use the DataCore Refresh button and check the status message.
Rule: Read the error message first. Correct the source information before repeatedly clicking buttons.
🆔 Borrower ID & Loan ID ▶

These IDs help the system identify the borrower and the specific loan record.

Borrower Unique Number: Identifies the borrower/customer.

Loan Unique Number: Identifies the particular loan record.

💡 If an editable ID is changed during review, the reviewed value should be preserved when the record is posted.

Not every correction requires a complete re-extraction. Use the appropriate Refresh function.

🚦 Understanding the LendingOps Flow ▶

Understand what happens from the moment a report is pasted until the information reaches the operational records.

1 Paste Report

The daily operational report is placed into the workspace.

2 Extract

LendingOps reads the report and extracts the important values.

3 Validate

The system checks the report, date, market and historical information.

4 Reconcile

DataCore helps check customer information against the operational registry.

5 Review

Officers review the extracted information and correct permitted fields.

6 Process

Approved information is processed into the appropriate operational records.

Extract → Validate → Reconcile → Review → Process
